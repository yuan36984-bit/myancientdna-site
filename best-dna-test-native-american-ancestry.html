Our take

If you want a direct comparison to the ancient founding population and reference genomes like Anzick-1, MyHeritage's Ancient Origins is the most direct mainstream option — see what that reflects on our Native American ancestry page. For a modern regional breakdown or finding living relatives, 23andMe and AncestryDNA respectively are more commonly cited. Whichever you choose, treat percentages in this category with extra caution given the founding population's smaller genetic diversity relative to other world regions.

What "Native American ancestry" actually means in your results

Native American ancestry traces to a single founding migration across the Beringian land bridge near the end of the last Ice Age, following a period of genetic isolation known as the Beringian standstill. See our full Native American ancestry guide for the details, including why percentage estimates here can be less precise than for some other regions.

A note on tribal enrollment

No DNA test, from any provider, can establish tribal enrollment or citizenship. Enrollment is determined individually by each federally or state-recognized tribal nation based on its own criteria, which typically involve documented lineal descent rather than a DNA percentage. A DNA test result is not accepted as proof of enrollment by tribal nations.

Don't skip the free option

If you've already tested elsewhere, some providers let you upload existing raw DNA data at low or no cost rather than buying a second full kit. See our step-by-step testing guide for how that process generally works.

FAQ

Can a DNA test get me enrolled in a tribe?

No. Tribal enrollment is determined by individual tribal nations based on their own criteria, typically documented lineal descent, not a commercial DNA test result.

Why do Native American DNA percentages seem less precise than other ancestry categories?

The founding population went through a genetic bottleneck during the Beringian migration, which reduced genetic diversity compared to some other world regions, making fine-grained percentage estimates inherently harder across all providers.
