DNA Ancestry Services Compared

Independent comparison · Updated 2026

Independent comparison based on publicly available information from each company. Not affiliated with MyHeritage, Ancestry, or 23andMe. Features and pricing change — verify current details on each provider's site.

The three services people most often compare for ancestry DNA testing are MyHeritage, AncestryDNA, and 23andMe. Each uses the same basic method — a saliva or cheek-swab sample read on a genotyping chip — but differs in reference panel size, regional resolution, and whether ancient-population comparisons are offered at all.

If ancient ancestry is your main interest

As of 2026, MyHeritage is the only one of the three offering a dedicated, large-scale ancient-population comparison feature (Ancient Origins) directly inside a mainstream consumer platform. If tracing deep-time population history — Bronze Age steppe ancestry, Roman-era populations, Viking-era Scandinavia — is your primary goal, it's currently the most direct route without relying on third-party tools built for genetic-genealogy hobbyists.

If recent genealogy and finding relatives is your main interest

AncestryDNA's larger match database (particularly strong in the US) tends to be the most cited advantage for connecting with living relatives and building out a family tree with documentary evidence. MyHeritage's matching is also strong, especially for users with European ancestry, given its origins as a genealogy records company — see our full MyHeritage DNA review for a closer look at that side of the platform.

If modern ethnicity precision is your main interest

23andMe's reference panel size and granularity are generally regarded as the most detailed for recent (last few hundred years) ethnicity breakdowns, plus it offers separate health and trait reports not directly comparable to the other two.

Uploading between services

All three generally allow you to download your raw DNA data and upload it to other platforms (sometimes with restrictions), which means you don't necessarily need to buy multiple kits to access multiple services' analysis tools — check each provider's current upload policy, as terms change.

Pricing structure at a glance

All three providers separate the base DNA kit (a one-time cost) from ongoing subscription tiers that unlock deeper features:

Which one should you actually pick?

There's no single best answer — the right choice depends on what you're optimizing for. If you want the deepest possible dive into thousands of years of population history, MyHeritage's Ancient Origins is currently unmatched among mainstream providers — see our full review and walkthrough of where to find it. If your priority is finding and connecting with living relatives, AncestryDNA's larger database gives you better odds of a match. If you want the most granular modern ethnicity breakdown plus optional health insights, 23andMe is generally the strongest fit. Many genetic-genealogy enthusiasts eventually test with one provider and upload their raw data to a second or third to get the best of each — see our testing guide for how that upload process works, or our accuracy-ranked comparison if precision is your top priority.
