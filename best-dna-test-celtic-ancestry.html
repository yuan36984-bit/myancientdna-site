Why "Celtic" doesn't mean one genetic population

Historically, "Celtic" describes a group of related languages and a shared Iron Age material culture that spread across a huge area of Europe — not a single genetically uniform group. Modern popular usage narrowed the term to mean Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Cornwall, and Brittany specifically, but genetically, populations across that region and era were shaped by multiple waves of migration layered on top of each other, including the same Bronze Age steppe ancestry found across much of Europe. See our full Celtic ancestry guide for how that layered history shows up in a DNA result.

FAQ

Is LivingDNA or MyHeritage better for Celtic ancestry?

It depends on what you want. LivingDNA is stronger for a detailed modern regional breakdown within the British Isles. MyHeritage's Ancient Origins is stronger if you want a comparison to Iron Age Celtic-speaking archaeological populations across a broader ancient European context.

Does a high "Irish" or "Scottish" percentage mean I have Celtic ancestry?

Not necessarily in the deep-time sense. A modern Ireland or Scotland region reflects your resemblance to people living there today, whose ancestry includes Celtic-speaking populations but also earlier hunter-gatherer, farmer, and Bronze Age steppe ancestry layered over thousands of years. An ancient-origins feature that names an Iron Age Celtic category is a more direct comparison to that specific historical population.
