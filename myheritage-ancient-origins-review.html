MyHeritage Ancient Origins Review

Independent review · Not affiliated with MyHeritage · Updated 2026

This is an independent, unofficial review based on publicly available information about MyHeritage's product, including MyHeritage's own official announcement post. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by MyHeritage. For authoritative details and current pricing, always check MyHeritage's own site.

MyHeritage launched Ancient Origins in February 2025, developed in partnership with Illustrative DNA, a company specializing in ancient-DNA population analysis. It's positioned as a companion to MyHeritage's existing Ethnicity Estimate, extending the timeline from a few hundred years of modern ancestry back to roughly 10,000 years, comparing your DNA to more than 150 ancient civilizations and archaeological sample sets — groups like Imperial Romans, Norse Vikings, Phoenicians, and Ancient Egyptians.

What it actually shows

Where a standard ethnicity estimate answers "what modern populations does your DNA resemble," Ancient Origins answers a longer-range version of that question by comparing your genome directly to genetic material recovered from archaeological remains spanning the Neolithic period through the late Middle Ages. The result is typically presented as a breakdown of ancient population groups your DNA statistically resembles, rather than a modern country-level map.

Do you need to test again?

No. Ancient Origins is calculated from your existing MyHeritage DNA results — if you've already tested with MyHeritage, or uploaded raw DNA data from another company to MyHeritage, the feature runs against data you already have. There's no separate swab or second kit required.

Pricing and access

Ancient Origins is a premium feature gated behind MyHeritage's Complete or Omni subscription tiers — it isn't included with a free account or with the DNA kit purchase alone. Subscription pricing changes periodically, so check MyHeritage's pricing page directly for current rates rather than relying on a fixed number here. If budget is the deciding factor, see our cheapest DNA test roundup for how this compares to lower-cost alternatives.

Strengths

  • First mainstream consumer platform to offer high-resolution ancient-population comparisons at this scale, rather than a niche third-party tool.
  • Works from existing DNA data — no need to re-test if you already have results on MyHeritage or have uploaded a raw data file.
  • Broad reference set: 150+ ancient civilizations and archaeological samples, spanning roughly 10,000 years.

Limitations to know before you subscribe

  • Requires an ongoing paid subscription (Complete or Omni), not a one-time purchase — factor that into the real cost.
  • Like all ancient-DNA comparisons, results describe statistical resemblance to sampled populations, not a documented, unbroken family lineage.
  • Reference panels and resulting percentages can shift over time as more ancient samples are added — treat results as a snapshot, not a fixed fact.

Who it's worth it for

If you're already a MyHeritage DNA user curious about deep-time ancestry beyond the standard ethnicity breakdown, or you're a genetic-genealogy hobbyist who enjoys this kind of analysis, it's a distinctive feature not widely matched elsewhere in mainstream consumer testing. If your main interest is recent genealogy — building a family tree, finding living relatives, documenting the last few generations — a standard ethnicity estimate and DNA matching (covered in our broader MyHeritage DNA review) will matter more than the ancient add-on.

The science behind Ancient Origins: PCA and "DeepAncestry Coordinates"

MyHeritage doesn't build its ancient-DNA reference panel from scratch. Like most consumer ancient-ancestry features, Ancient Origins draws on published academic ancient-DNA research — genomes sequenced from archaeological remains by research groups such as the Reich Lab, and made publicly available through resources like the Allen Ancient DNA Resource (see our explainer on where this reference data comes from). According to MyHeritage's own technical description of the product, Ancient Origins reports are generated using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), a statistical method for measuring genetic distance using multi-dimensional vectors. With your consent, your raw DNA data is first processed into what MyHeritage calls "DeepAncestry Coordinates" — a 25-dimensional vector derived by comparing your data to a reference set of modern and ancient populations. Those anonymized coordinates, not your raw DNA, are what's used to generate the actual reports. This PCA-based approach is somewhat distinct from the "admixture modeling" technique (software like ADMIXTURE or qpAdm) that some other ancient-DNA tools use — both aim to place your DNA in the context of ancient reference populations, but the underlying statistics differ.

What is genetic admixture, and why does it show up in the results anyway?

Even though MyHeritage's specific calculation method is PCA-based rather than classic admixture modeling, the underlying biological concept — genetic admixture — is still what the report is ultimately describing. "Admixture" means your DNA is a statistical blend of several ancestral source populations rather than a single unmixed group. So a result showing, for example, roughly 35% steppe-pastoralist-related ancestry, 30% early-farmer-related ancestry, and 20% hunter-gatherer-related ancestry isn't saying your ancestors belonged to three separate, unmixed tribes — it's describing which ancient reference populations, combined in those rough proportions, best explain the genetic variation the analysis sees in your sample. Nearly every present-day human population is itself the product of repeated ancient admixture events, which is part of why ancient-origins reports rarely show 100% of any single ancient group.

Common questions about MyHeritage Ancient Origins

The answers below cover the questions specific to this feature. For broader questions about ancient DNA testing in general, see our full FAQ page.

My percentages don't match what I expected — is something wrong?

Not necessarily. Ancient-origins percentages are statistical estimates, not certainties, and small components (especially under 5%) are the most sensitive to reference-panel gaps and modeling noise. It's also normal for results to shift slightly as MyHeritage and Illustrative DNA add more ancient samples to the reference panel over time. If a result seems way off from documented family history, it's worth reading the population descriptions rather than just the label — see how to interpret your results below.

How is the comparison actually calculated?

In short: your raw DNA is processed (with your consent) into a 25-dimensional "DeepAncestry Coordinates" vector using Principal Component Analysis, then compared statistically against a panel of ancient and modern reference populations. See the science section above for more detail, and our broader ancient DNA explainer for how this differs from a standard ethnicity estimate.

What exactly is included when I unlock Ancient Origins?

According to MyHeritage, the product includes five separate reports covering everything from a straightforward percentage breakdown to genetic distance maps — see the five Ancient Origins reports below for what each one shows. It all runs against DNA data you already have — see do you need to test again above.

Can Ancient Origins help me find living relatives?

No — that's a separate feature. Finding living relatives is handled by MyHeritage's DNA Matches tool, which compares your DNA to other users rather than to ancient archaeological samples. See our full MyHeritage DNA review for how DNA Matches works.

Can I cancel my subscription after viewing my results?

Subscription terms, billing cycles, and cancellation policies are set and managed directly by MyHeritage through your account settings — check their current terms before subscribing if this matters to your decision, since policies can change.

How do I actually get started?

You need a MyHeritage account with DNA data on file (either tested directly with MyHeritage or uploaded from another provider — see our testing guide) plus a Complete or Omni subscription. Once that's in place, see our walkthrough of where the Ancient Origins panel lives in the dashboard.

How you get your results: from consent to report

Getting your Ancient Origins reports isn't instant, and it isn't fully automatic either. Based on MyHeritage's own description of the process: after you open Ancient Origins from the DNA menu and confirm you have a Complete or Omni subscription, you're asked to give explicit consent for your DNA data to be processed for this feature — it's opt-in, not something that runs automatically on every DNA kit. You're then asked to select the origin that best represents you (one dominant ethnicity if you have one, or "Global" if you don't or aren't sure), which helps the underlying analysis focus on the most relevant reference samples. After that, reports are generated — MyHeritage states this is usually well within 24 hours, with an email notification once they're ready.

The five Ancient Origins reports

Ancient Origins isn't a single chart — it's a set of five distinct reports, each answering a slightly different question about your ancient ancestry.

Ancient Origins Breakdown

The main report: a percentage breakdown of the ancient populations your DNA is modeled against, shown alongside a map of where each population lived. You can move a slider to see the breakdown change across four historical eras — Bronze Age, Iron Age, Roman Era, and the Middle Ages — and there's an option to "tailor the model" if you want to try a different starting origin than the one you first selected.

Hunter-Gatherer and Farmer Breakdown

A narrower, single-question report showing what percentage of your DNA traces back to early hunter-gatherer populations versus early farming populations — two of the broadest and most consequential lineages in post-Ice-Age European and Near Eastern prehistory.

Genetic Similarities Breakdown

A more advanced view that compares your DNA against the entire reference database (rather than a single tailored model) and returns the top ancient populations, pairs of populations, or trios of populations that are the closest genetic match — useful if you want to see the full field of candidates rather than just one model's best guess.

Genetic Distance Maps

A visual plot (built on the same PCA method used to generate your DeepAncestry Coordinates) that places you, and every ancient reference population, as points in genetic space. Populations that cluster near each other on the plot are genetically similar to each other; where "You" land on the map shows which ancient populations you're closest to.

Sample Database

A reference encyclopedia of the ancient DNA samples and populations behind the other four reports — filterable by sample type or location, with details like time period, sample count, and haplogroups for readers who want to dig into the underlying research rather than just the summary percentages.

Understanding your Genetic Fit score

Alongside your percentage breakdown, MyHeritage displays a Genetic Fit score — a number indicating how closely your DNA matches the ancient populations used in that particular model. A lower score means a closer match to the reference populations; it isn't a percentage and shouldn't be read as one. Because the Genetic Fit score is calculated per historical era, it's normal to see it change as you move the era slider — some eras will fit your DNA more closely than others, which itself can be informative about which period the algorithm has the strongest reference data for.

Privacy: what happens to your DNA data

Ancient Origins is opt-in — MyHeritage states that results are only generated after you explicitly consent, and the feature doesn't run automatically just because you have a qualifying subscription. According to MyHeritage, your raw genetic data itself is never transferred to Illustrative DNA; only the anonymized DeepAncestry Coordinates vector is shared, and MyHeritage states that Illustrative DNA deletes it at the end of each session. If you change your mind after opting in, MyHeritage's stated process for removing your results is: go to "Manage DNA kits" from the DNA menu, open the three-dot menu next to the relevant DNA kit, and select "Delete Ancient Origin reports." As with any privacy claim, treat this summary as a starting point and verify current practice in MyHeritage's own privacy policy before relying on it.

How to interpret your Ancient Origins results

The results page typically presents a percentage breakdown across named ancient population groups, each tied to a specific archaeological sample set and time period. A few practical tips for reading it:

  • Look at the biggest components first. Small percentages (under 5%) are more sensitive to noise and reference-panel gaps than large, dominant components.
  • Read the population descriptions, not just the label. MyHeritage typically links each ancient group to a short description of the archaeological samples and time period behind it — this context matters more than the label alone.
  • Move the era slider before drawing conclusions. Your breakdown during the Bronze Age can look very different from your breakdown during the Middle Ages — both are "correct," they're just answering the question at different points in history.
  • Compare against your standard Ethnicity Estimate. The two reports use different reference panels (modern vs. ancient), so seeing how they relate to each other can be more informative than reading either in isolation.
  • Expect updates over time. As MyHeritage and Illustrative DNA add more ancient samples to the reference panel, your breakdown may shift slightly on recalculation — this is normal and reflects a growing dataset, not an error.

Typical user experience

Because Ancient Origins runs against existing DNA data, most users see results within minutes of enabling the feature (as opposed to the multi-week wait for an initial DNA kit to be processed). The interface generally presents results as a percentage chart alongside short descriptions of each ancient population, with some versions including a map or timeline view showing when and where each ancestral component is believed to have been present.
