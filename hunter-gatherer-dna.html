Hunter-Gatherer Ancestry: What It Means

Ancient population guide · Updated 2026

Hunter-gatherer ancestry is the deepest layer in most Ancient Origins breakdowns — it traces back to the people who lived in Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas for tens of thousands of years before farming existed. MyHeritage's Ancient Origins feature includes a dedicated Hunter-Gatherer and Farmer breakdown report precisely because this split marks one of the biggest turning points in human population history.

Also calledMesolithic ancestry, pre-agricultural ancestry (in Europe: Western, Eastern, or Scandinavian Hunter-Gatherer)
Time periodTens of thousands of years ago through roughly 6000 BCE in Europe, later or earlier elsewhere
LifestyleForaging, hunting, and fishing rather than farming or herding
Famous exampleCheddar Man, a roughly 10,000-year-old skeleton found in Britain
In modern populationsA minority but detectable component almost everywhere farming eventually spread

Who were the hunter-gatherers?

Before agriculture existed anywhere, every human population survived by foraging wild plants, hunting animals, and fishing — a way of life that persisted for the vast majority of our species' history. In Europe, geneticists distinguish between several regional hunter-gatherer groups, including Western Hunter-Gatherers (WHG), who left remains like the famous Cheddar Man found in Somerset, England, and Eastern Hunter-Gatherers (EHG) from further into Russia and the steppe. One striking finding from ancient-DNA analysis of Cheddar Man, published by London's Natural History Museum, was that he likely had dark skin and blue eyes — a combination that doesn't match common assumptions about what ancient Europeans looked like, and a good illustration of why ancient DNA sometimes overturns intuitive guesses.

How hunter-gatherer ancestry shows up in modern populations

When farming spread into Europe from Anatolia starting around 6000 BCE, incoming farmers gradually mixed with the hunter-gatherer populations already living there rather than replacing them outright everywhere. As a result, most modern Europeans carry a mix of hunter-gatherer and farmer ancestry, with the exact ratio varying by region — hunter-gatherer ancestry tends to be proportionally higher in parts of Northern and Eastern Europe, including the Baltic region and Scandinavia, and lower in the Mediterranean, where early farming populations left an especially strong and long-lasting genetic signature.

If your Ancient Origins results show hunter-gatherer ancestry

A hunter-gatherer signal in your results reflects resemblance to these deep pre-agricultural populations, layered underneath whatever farmer, steppe, or later ancestry your results also show. It's rarely the dominant component for most test-takers today, since farming populations and later Bronze Age migrations reshaped much of the gene pool over thousands of years — but it's almost always present in some proportion. Hunter-Gatherer ancestry vs. Early European Farmer ancestry

This is the core comparison MyHeritage's dedicated breakdown report is built around. Early European Farmer ancestry traces to Anatolian populations who migrated into Europe and introduced agriculture; hunter-gatherer ancestry traces to the populations already there. Neither one "replaced" the other completely — modern Europeans are a blend of both, plus later steppe-related ancestry layered on top during the Bronze Age. The relative balance of hunter-gatherer versus farmer ancestry is one of the oldest and most fundamental splits any ancient-DNA comparison can reveal.

Does a low hunter-gatherer percentage mean my ancestors weren't in Europe that long ago?

Not necessarily. Farming populations that arrived later often became the majority genetic contributor in a region even where hunter-gatherers had lived for tens of thousands of years beforehand, because of differences in population density and growth between foraging and farming societies. A low percentage reflects the genetic outcome of that demographic shift, not an absence of earlier human presence in the region.

Is hunter-gatherer ancestry only relevant to Europe?

No — hunter-gatherer populations existed everywhere before agriculture developed independently in different parts of the world. MyHeritage's Hunter-Gatherer and Farmer breakdown report is framed around this global transition, though the best-studied ancient reference samples tend to be European, given how much ancient-DNA research has focused on that region.
