Our take

If you want to see how your DNA compares to the deep ancestral lineages behind East Asian populations — not just a modern country-level estimate — MyHeritage's Ancient Origins is the most direct mainstream option with a named ancient-population category for the region. See what that comparison actually reflects on our East Asian ancient ancestry page. If your priority is the most granular modern-day regional breakdown, 23andMe's East Asian sub-categories are more commonly cited for that purpose, and if it's finding living relatives, AncestryDNA's larger database is the usual advantage.

What "East Asian ancestry" actually means in your results

Most East Asian ancestry, whether reported as a modern region or an ancient-origins category, traces back to a mixture of two deep ancestral lineages associated with the Yellow River and Amur River basins, later shaped by regional migrations and, in Japan specifically, additional Jōmon hunter-gatherer ancestry. A result naming a modern country reflects your resemblance to present-day reference populations from that country; an ancient-origins result reflects direct comparison to archaeological genomes. FAQ

Why do my East Asian percentages differ between providers?

Each provider builds its own reference panel and algorithm, so the same raw DNA can produce somewhat different regional breakdowns between companies. This is normal and expected rather than a sign that one company is wrong.

Does MyHeritage break down East Asian ancestry by country?

MyHeritage offers both a modern regional ethnicity estimate and a separate Ancient Origins comparison to ancient East Asian population categories; for country-level modern granularity specifically, 23andMe is more commonly cited as offering finer resolution.
