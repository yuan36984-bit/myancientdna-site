Cheapest DNA Ancestry Test in 2026

Independent comparison · Updated 2026

Independent comparison based on publicly available information from each company. Not affiliated with MyHeritage, Ancestry, or FamilyTreeDNA. Prices change frequently and vary by region and promotion — verify current pricing on each provider's site before buying.

Base DNA kit prices across the major providers are closer together than people expect — the bigger cost differences usually come from optional subscriptions layered on top, not the initial kit. It's also worth knowing that going cheaper than the established providers isn't free: unusually low-cost tests can mean a smaller, less rigorous reference panel and a real risk of an inaccurate result.

Our take

If you specifically want a full ethnicity percentage breakdown, MyHeritage's $89 base kit is the least expensive way to get one from a major, established provider, and it's also the cheapest path to an ancient-origins comparison if you later add the Complete/Omni subscription — see our full MyHeritage DNA review for what the base kit versus subscription tiers actually unlock. FamilyTreeDNA's $79 is technically cheaper, but it only tests one specific ancestral line (paternal or maternal, not both, and not a percentage-based estimate at all) — it's a different product for a different question, not a budget version of the same thing. AncestryDNA's $99 is the highest base price of the three here, offset by the largest region count and match database.

Why "too cheap" can be a red flag

Reputable providers spend heavily on building and continually updating large, diverse reference panels — that cost is part of what you're paying for. A DNA test priced well below the established providers may be using a smaller, outdated, or less rigorously validated reference panel, which can produce meaningfully less accurate ethnicity results even though the underlying lab process (reading your DNA sample) is similar across providers. If a deal looks unusually cheap for a full ancestry test, that's worth treating as a caution sign rather than pure savings.

Don't buy a second kit if you don't need one

If you've already tested with one provider, you often don't need to buy a second full kit to try another service's ancestry tools — most major providers let you download your raw DNA data and upload it elsewhere, sometimes for free or at a lower cost than a new kit. See our step-by-step testing guide for how that upload process generally works, and check each provider's current policy before assuming it's supported.

FAQ

What is the cheapest DNA test that includes an ethnicity percentage?

Among major, established providers, MyHeritage's base kit is typically the lowest-priced option that includes a full autosomal ethnicity estimate. Prices change and vary by promotion, so check current pricing directly before buying.

Is a cheaper DNA test less accurate?

Not automatically — the established mainstream providers are priced fairly close together and all maintain substantial reference panels. The bigger risk is with unusually cheap DNA tests well below the mainstream price range, which may use smaller or less rigorously validated reference panels.

Do I need to pay for a subscription to see my ethnicity results?

Usually the base ethnicity estimate is included with the one-time kit purchase, but some deeper features are gated behind a subscription — for example, MyHeritage's Ancient Origins requires a Complete or Omni subscription. Check what's included in the base price versus the upsell before buying.
