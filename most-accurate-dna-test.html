Most Accurate DNA Ancestry Test in 2026

Independent comparison · Updated 2026

Independent comparison based on publicly available information from each company. Not affiliated with MyHeritage, Ancestry, or 23andMe. Features and pricing change — verify current details on each provider's site before buying.

"Most accurate" doesn't have one single answer, because DNA testing companies measure different things and there's no independent, universally accepted accuracy benchmark that scores them all on the same scale. What you actually get is a few concrete, comparable factors: reference panel size and how recently it was updated, number of ethnicity regions, and match-database size for finding relatives. Here's how the main providers stack up on each.

Region count isn't the same as accuracy

23andMe's 4,500+ regions is the largest number on this page, but a higher region count reflects how finely a company subdivides its map, not necessarily how confidently it can place your DNA in the correct one. A company can report more regions by splitting existing categories into smaller pieces without actually improving the underlying statistical confidence. The more meaningful accuracy signals are: how recently the reference panel was updated (older panels drift out of date as population genetics research improves), how large and geographically diverse the reference panel is, and — separately — how large the match database is if finding living relatives matters to you.

Our take

For raw match-database size, which functions as a rough proxy for how many potential DNA relatives you'll be connected to, AncestryDNA is the largest of the three. For the most recently refreshed reference panel with meaningfully finer regional detail outside North America, MyHeritage's 2025 update is the more current option — and it's the only one of the three offering a dedicated ancient-population comparison feature going back thousands of years rather than only a modern-region breakdown. For the single largest region count plus separate health and trait reporting, 23andMe is the strongest fit. None of these is objectively "most accurate" across every dimension — see our full side-by-side comparison for how they differ on ancient-origins depth specifically, which none of the three major competitor "accuracy" roundups typically cover in depth.

What accuracy doesn't mean

None of these estimates should be read as a precise, documented genealogical record. Ethnicity percentages are statistical estimates based on comparison to a reference panel, and results for the same person can shift over time as panels are updated, or differ slightly between companies using different methods — see our general FAQ for more on why that happens and what a percentage actually represents.

FAQ

Which DNA test has the most accurate ethnicity estimate?

There's no single independently verified answer. AncestryDNA has the largest match database, MyHeritage has the most recently refreshed reference panel (as of its 2025 update) plus a unique deep-time ancient-origins feature, and 23andMe has the largest number of geographic regions. Which one is "most accurate" depends on which of those factors matters most to you.

Does a bigger reference panel always mean better accuracy?

Generally, yes — a larger, more diverse, and more recently updated reference panel tends to improve confidence, especially for regions outside North America and Western Europe that were historically underrepresented in early panels. But panel size alone doesn't guarantee accuracy; how the panel is built and maintained also matters.
