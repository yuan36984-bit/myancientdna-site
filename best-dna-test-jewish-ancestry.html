Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry is one of the more genetically distinctive populations a consumer DNA test can detect, thanks to a well-documented population bottleneck a few centuries ago that left a strong, identifiable genetic signature — see our full explainer for how that history shaped the DNA itself. Because the signal is so clear, most major providers detect it reliably even at fairly low percentages, so the real differences between services come down to genealogy records, community size, and whether ancient-population comparisons are offered.

Our take

All four services listed here reliably detect Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry — this isn't a case where one provider clearly outperforms the others on raw detection. The decision comes down to what you want to do with the result. If you want your DNA match paired with the deepest genealogical record search specifically for Jewish family history, MyHeritage's records collection and its Ancient Origins feature (which frames the result in a deep-time population context rather than only a modern-region percentage) is a strong combination — see our full breakdown of what that specific category shows on the Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry page. If your priority is the largest pool of potential DNA relative matches, AncestryDNA's database size is the more commonly cited advantage. If you're interested in tracing a single paternal or maternal line — for example, researching a possible priestly (Cohanim) lineage — FamilyTreeDNA's dedicated Y-DNA testing is built specifically for that, though it only covers one of your many ancestral lines.

Why the Ashkenazi Jewish signal is so distinctive

Ashkenazi Jewish populations trace largely to a relatively small founding group in medieval Europe that remained genetically distinct due to limited intermarriage with surrounding populations for centuries — a population geneticist would call this a "founder effect" or "bottleneck." That history left recognizable genetic markers that reference panels can detect with unusual confidence compared to some other regional ancestries, which is part of why it shows up reliably even in people with only one Ashkenazi Jewish grandparent or great-grandparent. For the deeper history behind that pattern, see our dedicated explainer.

FAQ

Which DNA test is most accurate for Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry?

Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry is detected reliably across MyHeritage, 23andMe, and AncestryDNA due to its distinctive genetic signature. There isn't strong independent evidence that one clearly outperforms the others on raw detection accuracy — the better differentiator is which service's genealogy records and features fit what you actually want to do with the result.

Can a DNA test confirm I'm a Cohen (Kohen)?

A Y-DNA test can show whether you carry the Cohen Modal Haplotype, a Y-chromosome marker pattern found at elevated frequency among men with a Jewish priestly (Cohanim) tradition in published research. It's a statistical association from population studies, not a religious or legal confirmation of Cohanim status, and it only reflects your direct paternal line.

Why does my percentage of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry differ slightly between services?

Each company uses its own reference panel and statistical model, so the same raw DNA can produce slightly different percentages between providers. This is normal and expected — see our general FAQ for more on why ancestry estimates vary between companies.
