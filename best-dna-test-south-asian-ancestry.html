Our take

If you want a direct comparison to the ancient ancestral lineages behind South Asian populations — the ANI/ASI model built from sites like Rakhigarhi — MyHeritage's Ancient Origins is the most direct mainstream option. See what that comparison actually reflects on our South Asian & Indus Valley ancestry page. For the most granular modern-day regional breakdown, 23andMe is more commonly cited, and for finding living relatives, AncestryDNA's larger database is the usual advantage.

What "South Asian ancestry" actually means in your results

Most South Asian ancestry results, ancient or modern, trace back to a mixture of Ancestral North Indian (ANI) and Ancestral South Indian (ASI) ancestry, later shaped by the Bronze Age steppe migration into the region. A modern regional result reflects comparison to present-day populations from a specific area; an ancient-origins result reflects direct comparison to archaeological genomes. See our full South Asian ancient ancestry guide for the details.

FAQ

Why do South Asian ancestry percentages vary so much between providers?

Each provider builds its own reference panel, and South Asia's genetic diversity is substantial, so companies vary widely in how finely they subdivide the region. This is a normal difference in methodology, not an error.

Does an ancient-origins test show my caste or specific regional group?

No. Ancient-origins comparisons work at the level of broad ancestral population categories (like ANI/ASI-derived ancestry), not modern social or caste categories, which aren't a genetic classification.
