Haplogroup J2: Origins and Ancient DNA History

Y-DNA haplogroup guide · Updated 2026

Haplogroup J2 is closely tied to the Near East and the spread of early agriculture, making it one of the clearest Y-DNA signals of the Neolithic farming expansion into Europe and beyond.

At a glance

TypeY-DNA (paternal line)
Estimated ageDiversified roughly 15,000–20,000 years ago in the Near East, with major expansion during the Neolithic
Modern peak frequencyAnatolia, the Caucasus, the Levant, Mesopotamia, and parts of the Mediterranean and South Asia
Key ancient associationEarly Near Eastern farming populations and their westward Neolithic expansion

A Near Eastern origin

J2 is believed to have diversified in or near the Fertile Crescent, the region where agriculture first developed. As farming populations expanded outward from this region roughly 8,000–9,000 years ago, they carried J2 (alongside other lineages like haplogroup G) into Europe, gradually mixing with resident hunter-gatherer populations. See our Early European Farmer ancestry guide for the broader migration story.

A lasting Mediterranean signature

Today J2 remains most common around the Mediterranean basin, the Caucasus, and parts of the Near East and South Asia, reflecting both its original range and the reach of later Bronze Age and Classical-era population movements, including those touched on in our Roman-era ancestry guide.

What carrying J2 means — and doesn't mean

A J2 result reflects a single ancient paternal line associated with the Near East and the spread of farming, not a complete ancestry picture. See our full explainer on how Y-DNA and mtDNA haplogroups work for context.

FAQ

Is J2 the same as haplogroup J1?

No. J1 and J2 are separate branches of the broader J haplogroup with different geographic histories — J1 is more associated with the Arabian Peninsula and parts of the Near East, while J2 is more closely tied to the Neolithic farming expansion.

Does J2 mean I have ancient farmer ancestry?

It suggests your direct paternal line likely traces to Near Eastern populations associated with early agriculture, but your overall genome-wide ancestry could include many other ancestral sources as well.
