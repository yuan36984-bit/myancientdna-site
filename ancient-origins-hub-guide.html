Where to Find Your Ancient Origins Results

Walkthrough · Independent guide

This is an independent walkthrough, not an official support page. Menu labels and navigation inside any provider's site can change — if something here doesn't match what you see, check the provider's own help center.

If you already have DNA results with a provider that offers ancient-population comparisons, the results usually live in a dedicated section of your DNA dashboard rather than mixed into your standard ethnicity page. Here's the general path and what each part of the report typically shows.

1. Log in and go to your DNA dashboard

Start from your account's main DNA section (not the general family-tree or records area). This is usually where your ethnicity estimate, DNA matches, and any premium ancestry add-ons are listed together.

2. Look for the ancient-origins or "ancient" tab specifically

Because ancient-population comparison is typically a premium, separate feature from the standard ethnicity estimate, it's usually labeled distinctly — look for a tab or card explicitly mentioning "Ancient Origins" or "ancient" ancestry rather than assuming it's folded into the default ethnicity breakdown.

3. Check subscription status if it's not visible

If you don't see an ancient-origins option at all, the most common reason is subscription tier — this kind of feature is usually gated behind a premium plan rather than included with a free account or a one-time DNA kit purchase. Check your account's subscription or billing page to confirm what tier you're on. We break down exactly which tiers unlock it in our Ancient Origins review.

4. Reading the results page

Once you're on the ancient-origins results page, you'll typically find a few standard sections:

  • A percentage breakdown of ancient population groups your DNA statistically resembles.
  • Named historical/archaeological groups (e.g., specific time periods and regions) rather than modern country names — see what a match to Bronze Age steppe ancestry or Viking-era Scandinavian ancestry actually means.
  • Supporting context — often a short explanation of each ancient population and the archaeological samples behind it.

For a deeper explanation of what these percentages actually mean statistically, see our ancient DNA explainer.

If you don't have results yet

You'll need either a new DNA kit or, if you've already tested with another provider, an uploaded raw DNA file — see our testing guide for the step-by-step process before you'll have anything to view here.

Troubleshooting: results not showing up

If you've confirmed your subscription tier includes the feature and you still don't see an ancient-origins panel, a few things are worth checking before contacting support:

  • Processing delay. If you just uploaded raw DNA data or completed a new kit, allow some time for initial processing to finish before the ancient-origins comparison becomes available.
  • Browser cache. Account and subscription changes sometimes take a page refresh or a fresh login to reflect in the dashboard.
  • Data quality flags. Occasionally a raw DNA file uploaded from another provider doesn't meet the minimum marker overlap needed for a reliable ancient-population comparison — check for any data-quality notice on your DNA results page.
  • Regional availability. Some premium features roll out region by region before becoming globally available — this is less common but worth ruling out if everything else checks out.
