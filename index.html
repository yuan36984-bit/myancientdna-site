Ancient DNA science, explained

Beyond the "Ancient Origins" population breakdown, these are the questions readers ask most about ancient DNA generally — what it reveals about extinct human relatives, deep single-line ancestry, and the scientific records behind it all.

How much Neanderthal DNA do I have?

Most people outside sub-Saharan Africa carry roughly 1-2% Neanderthal DNA, inherited from interbreeding that happened as early Homo sapiens migrated out of Africa some 50,000-60,000 years ago. The specific segments you carry matter more than the percentage itself. Read the full explainer →

What is Denisovan DNA?

A separate group of archaic humans, identified almost entirely from a single Siberian finger bone, left its strongest genetic trace in Melanesian populations today — as much as 4-6% of the genome, far higher than anywhere else in the world. Read the full explainer →

What is a haplogroup?

A haplogroup traces a single line of ancestry — either strictly paternal (Y-DNA) or strictly maternal (mtDNA) — going back tens of thousands of years. It's a very different measurement from a percentage-based ancestry breakdown, which blends all of your ancestral lines at once. Read the full explainer →

What's the oldest DNA ever found?

The current record is roughly 2 million years old, recovered from ancient sediment in Greenland — far beyond the roughly 10,000-year window covered by consumer ancient-origins tests. Read the full explainer →

Which famous historical figures have had their DNA sequenced?

Ötzi the Iceman, Cheddar Man, King Richard III, and Tutankhamun are among the best-known cases — each revealing surprising details about appearance, health, family, or cause of death that historical records alone couldn't answer. Read the full explainer →

Common questions

A few of the questions readers ask most before testing or paying for an ancient-ancestry add-on. The full list, with more detail, is on the FAQ page.

What is ancient DNA ancestry testing?

It compares your genetic data to DNA recovered from archaeological remains dated to specific historical periods, showing which sampled ancient populations your DNA most closely resembles — unlike a standard ethnicity estimate, which compares you to modern reference populations.

How accurate are the results?

They're statistical estimates based on comparison to available ancient DNA reference samples. Results can shift over time as reference panels grow, and they describe population-level resemblance, not a documented, unbroken family lineage.

Is ancient DNA testing free?

It depends on the provider. Some ancient-origins features, such as MyHeritage's Ancient Origins, are gated behind a premium subscription tier rather than included with a free account or one-time kit purchase.
